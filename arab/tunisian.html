Pronunciation

Lesson 0: The Arabic Alphabet

Modern Standard Arabic and Tunisian Derja are written with the Arabic alphabet, which has 28 basic letters written from right to left. Each letter can have up to four shapes: isolated, initial, medial, and final, depending on its position in the word.

The table below shows each letter in its isolated form, and its typical shapes at the beginning, middle, and end of a word, together with its common name and approximate sound.

Tunisians often mix Arabic script and the Latin “chat alphabet” (Arabizi). The next pronunciation lessons show how these special sounds are written and typed.

Lesson 1: Difficult Arabic Sounds

Derja shares many sounds with Standard Arabic, but also has unique Mediterranean influences. Mastering these “difficult” sounds is the key to speaking naturally.

Sound/Letter Description Example
ح Deep H (Ha). Stronger and deeper than a standard H. Exhale strongly from the throat. Hmema (Pigeon)
ع 'Ayn. A guttural sound formed deep in the throat. A very distinct Arabic sound. 'Ayshek (Thank you)
ق Qaf. A deep guttural K sound. Sometimes sounds like a powerful “glottal stop”. Qahwa (Coffee)
غ Ghayn. Sounds like the French “r”, a gargling sound. Gheli (Expensive)

Lesson 2: The Chat Alphabet (Arabizi)

When Tunisians text or use social media, they rarely use Arabic script. Instead, they use a clever mix of Latin letters and numbers to represent distinct Arabic sounds that do not exist in English or French. This is called Arabizi or the “Chat Alphabet”.

Important note: The word S7i7 that appears in the quiz is a perfect example. The number 7 represents the letter ح (deep Ha), because the shape of the number 7 resembles the Arabic letter.

Number Arabic Sound Use Example
3 ع 3aslema (Hello)
5 خ 5obz (Bread)
7 ح S7i7 (Correct)
9 ق 9ahwa (Coffee)
